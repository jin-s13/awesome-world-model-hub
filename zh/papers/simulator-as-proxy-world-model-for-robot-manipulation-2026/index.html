Simulator-as-Proxy World Model for Robot Manipulation

arXiv 2026 63.7 method, application

TLDR

SimPWM combines a parameter-free rule-based simulator and a video model to render proxy robot/object states, improving arm and object prediction on DROID episodes.

评分理由

The paper introduces a clever decoupling of explicit simulated state from generative video rendering, enabling controlled tests of proxy-video agreement. Its strengths include novel perturbation of simulated objects and strong empirical results on real DROID episodes; weaknesses include reliance on rule-based simulation and inconsistent object-state adherence across base video models.

深度分析

创新点

  • Introduces SimPWM, a simulator-as-proxy world model that reconstructs the robot as a 3D model and touched objects as 3D points from the first frame alone using a calibrated camera.
  • Uses a rule-based, parameter-free simulator to move the explicit proxy state under actions and a video model to render the result, returning both realistic video and simulated state.
  • Provides an explicit, perturbable proxy state to test the assumption that video world models show what the proxy shows, including freezing simulated objects while the arm follows recorded motions.
  • Uses action perturbations and disagreement between video and simulated state to reveal when the video ignores the simulated state.

方法

Given a calibrated camera, SimPWM represents the robot by its 3D model and the objects the gripper may touch as 3D points from the first frame alone; a rule-based simulator with no trained parameters moves them under actions, and a video model renders the result. It is evaluated on DROID episodes from unseen setups with three base video models, using interventions that freeze simulated objects while the arm moves as recorded. Metrics include robot IoU, missed pick-up rate, and object lift rate under frozen objects and perturbed actions.

关键结果

Across three base video models, the arm follows the proxy with robot IoU 0.75-0.78, versus at most 0.69 for end-to-end models, and simulated objects reduce missed pick-ups from 54% to 38% over a robot-only proxy. Freezing rigid objects lowers MiniMax-H3 lift rate from 42% to 10% but LTX-2.3 only from 57% to 49%, and more than half of MiniMax-H3's remaining lifts are cloth and rope carried rigidly.

局限性

  • The video follows the simulated object state only with some base models; LTX-2.3 appears to infer grasp from arm motion rather than the simulated object state.
  • The simulator handles cloth and rope as rigid objects, and more than half of MiniMax-H3's remaining object lifts are of these deformable objects.
  • Under perturbed actions, video can disagree with the recorded simulated state, revealing that the video may ignore the simulated state.
