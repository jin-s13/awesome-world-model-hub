Compact Visuotactile World Models for Lifting: Prediction, Reward Alignment, and Force Constraints

arXiv 2026 45 method, application

TLDR

Compact visuotactile world models improve force-constrained lifting control via model-assisted feedback, but not imagined RL; evaluated extensively in MuJoCo without physical robots.

Reasoning

Strengths include systematic evaluation across many policies and environments, confidence intervals, and a compact model. Weaknesses are the lack of physical-robot transfer, no closed-loop safety guarantee, and the finding that imagined RL underperforms reactive IQL, limiting practical impact.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Compact 652,157-parameter action-conditioned visuotactile world model for rigid-box lifting under force constraints.
  • Model-assisted force feedback using learned visuotactile dynamics to improve force-budgeted success.
  • Fixed evaluation protocol spanning 34 policies, 120 fresh MuJoCo environments with geometry/physical-parameter shifts, and 324 independently replayed action branches on 12 ID environments.
  • Empirical tactile-residual stress test with 330 additional executions.
  • Evidence that accurate tactile forecasts need not improve pooled force-constrained control.

Methodology

The study uses a 652,157-parameter action-conditioned visuotactile world model with matched behavior cloning, policy learning in imagination, independent reactive implicit Q-learning, and model-assisted force feedback. A fixed protocol executes 34 policies on 120 fresh MuJoCo environments with geometry and physical-parameter shifts, plus 324 independently replayed action branches on 12 additional in-distribution environments. Evaluation metrics include force action-effect MAE, force-budgeted success, and pooled joint success, with persistence and reactive IQL as comparison points.

Key Results

Visuotactile dynamics reduce force action-effect MAE from 0.413 N for persistence to 0.338 N. Model-assisted feedback raises in-distribution force-budgeted success from 73.3% to 93.3% (paired difference +20.0 pp, 95% CI [6.7, 33.4]) mainly during scripted lowering, while the pooled difference is +3.9 pp (95% CI [-4.5, 11.7]); imagined RL achieves 11.9% pooled joint success versus 25.0% for reactive IQL.

Limitations

  • Evidence is limited to rigid-box lifting after a common approach.
  • No physical-robot transfer is reported.
  • No closed-loop safety guarantee is provided.
  • The pooled benefit of model-assisted feedback is not statistically significant (95% CI includes zero).
  • Imagined RL underperforms reactive IQL (11.9% vs 25.0% pooled joint success).
