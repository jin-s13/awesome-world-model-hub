EVO-WAM: Evolving World Action Models through Video-Action Verification

arXiv 2026 40.2 method, system

TLDR

EVO-WAM adapts world action models to unseen robot tasks by learning from self-generated video-action trajectories, using verification to improve success in simulation and real world.

Reasoning

The paper presents a strong self-supervised framework that avoids external execution feedback and demonstrates significant gains on unseen RoboTwin and real-world tasks. Weaknesses include reliance on specific base models and limited details on failure cases or generalization beyond tested benchmarks.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • EVO-WAM framework adapts world action models to unseen tasks by learning from their own generated video-action trajectories without executing candidate actions in an external environment
  • Augmenting WAM training with state prediction and anchored multi-frame context to enable complete autoregressive rollouts without external execution feedback
  • Selecting task-completing prefixes via a vision-language model and verifying video-action consistency using an inverse dynamics model
  • Iteratively training the WAM on verified prefixes and generating new rollouts with the updated model

Methodology

EVO-WAM augments WAM training with state prediction and anchored multi-frame context, generates video-action rollouts, and filters them by selecting task-completing prefixes with a vision-language model and verifying video-action consistency with an inverse dynamics model. It iteratively trains on verified prefixes and regenerates rollouts with the updated model. The approach is evaluated on seven unseen RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and three real-world long-horizon composite tasks using average success rate.

Key Results

On seven unseen RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, EVO-WAM improves average success rates from 26.9% to 68.0% for Cosmos3 and from 28.5% to 46.4% for DreamZero. On three unseen real-world long-horizon composite tasks, it improves Cosmos3's average success rate from 20.0% to 76.7%.
