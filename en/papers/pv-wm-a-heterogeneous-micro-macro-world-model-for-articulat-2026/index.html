Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • History-only heterogeneous micro-macro world model over structured post-perception tracks for articulated pedestrian-vehicle co-rollout
  • Synchronized heterogeneous state that recurrently advances pedestrian root motion, 15-joint articulation, and learned vehicle states
  • Self-feeding recurrent rollout where generated pedestrian and vehicle chunks supply the next recurrent boundary, with vehicle boxes reconstructed from predicted center and heading using observed extent and P-V geometry recomputed after every transition
  • Single-network formulation that unifies heterogeneous future state while preserving type-specific pedestrian and vehicle dynamics

Methodology

PV-WM is a recurrent world model over structured post-perception tracks that advances pedestrian root motion, 15-joint articulation, and learned vehicle states in a synchronized heterogeneous state. Generated pedestrian and vehicle chunks become the next recurrent boundary; vehicle boxes are reconstructed from predicted center and heading using observed extent, and pedestrian-vehicle geometry is recomputed after each transition. Evaluation uses aligned Waymo contexts and compares against a matched one-shot complete-state predictor and a validation-selected Modular Specialist using pedestrian, vehicle, and pedestrian-vehicle metrics plus compute efficiency measures.

Key Results

Recurrent execution reduces Root ADE by 12.7% and MPJPE by 14.8% relative to a matched one-shot complete-state predictor, and across 824 aligned Waymo contexts with 797 valid future vehicle support, PV-WM reduces Root ADE by 5.2%, MPJPE by 7.6%, P-V distance error by 11.9%, and oriented-box closest-approach error by 5.8% relative to a validation-selected Modular Specialist. The single-network model uses 57.1% fewer parameters, 96.5% lower average FLOPs per local scene, and 25.5% lower measured p95 latency; feedback interventions show later predictions depend on generated articulation content, temporal order, and pedestrian identity.
