GAWM: A Compact Latent World-Action Model for Multi-Task Robot Manipulation

arXiv 2026 48.3 method, application

TLDR

GAWM is a compact latent world-action model using a frozen DINOv3 encoder and lightweight predictor to forecast future tokens for multi-task robot manipulation, outperforming billion-parameter models.

Reasoning

The paper presents a strong efficiency-focused contribution, showing competitive or superior performance with under 100M parameters on benchmarks and real robots. Weaknesses are that the abstract does not fully clarify the limitations of latent-space prediction or the absence of action-conditioned dynamics, and details of generalization are limited.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Proposes GAWM, a compact multi-task world-action model with fewer than 100M parameters, avoiding billion-parameter vision-language or video-generation backbones.
  • Predicts future observations in the latent space of a frozen DINOv3 encoder rather than in raw pixel or video space.
  • Compresses each camera view into a small set of tokens and uses a lightweight predictor conditioned on the task rather than on actions.
  • Forecasts latent tokens at two future horizons and supervises them with dense patch features of future frames to encode spatial layout.
  • Introduces an action decoder that directly regresses a chunk of future actions from current and predicted tokens.

Methodology

GAWM encodes camera views with a frozen DINOv3 encoder and compresses the features of each view into a small set of tokens. A lightweight task-conditioned predictor forecasts these tokens at two future horizons, supervised by the dense patch features of future frames. An action decoder regresses future action chunks from current and predicted tokens, and the model is evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and a dual-arm robot against billion-parameter WAMs and VLAs using metrics such as action-prediction error and inference latency.

Key Results

With fewer than 100M parameters and no robot or video pretraining, GAWM outperforms billion-parameter WAMs on RoboTwin 2.0 and performs comparably to billion-parameter VLAs on LIBERO. On a dual-arm robot, it achieves lower action-prediction error on held-out demonstrations than a fine-tuned billion-parameter VLA with substantially lower inference latency, and ablations show the latent world model improves performance under limited training data or time.
