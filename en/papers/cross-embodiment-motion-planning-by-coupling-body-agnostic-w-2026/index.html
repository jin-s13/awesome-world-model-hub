Cross-Embodiment Motion Planning by Coupling Body-Agnostic World Model with Differentiable Ego-Dynamics

arXiv 2026 34.4 method

TLDR

Hourglass factorizes cross-embodiment motion planning into a body-agnostic world model and differentiable ego-dynamics, training a generalist policy on seven simulated robots.

Reasoning

The paper presents a novel architecture that cleanly separates body-agnostic prediction from embodiment-specific dynamics, enabling pooled cross-embodiment training and body-aware imagination. Strengths include a principled factorization and comparative simulated results, but the evaluation is limited to simulation and depends on flat-ground and camera ego-motion assumptions.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Hourglass architecture that factorizes cross-embodiment motion planning at the camera ego-motion interface between a body-agnostic world model and robot-specific differentiable ego-dynamics.
  • Use of reduced-order differentiable ego-dynamics templates with identified physical parameters to map commands to body-specific camera ego-motion.
  • A generalist parameter-conditioned policy trained once at the world-model/ego-dynamics junction, with imagination rolling out each robot's template before the world model to make the policy body-aware.
  • Zero-shot transfer to a real robot by identifying ego-dynamics from three minutes of teleoperated driving without retraining the synthetic-data-trained model.

Methodology

The paper proposes Hourglass, which couples a body-agnostic world model with each robot's differentiable ego-dynamics only through camera ego-motion. The world model is trained on pooled cross-embodiment data, while each robot's ego-dynamics is a reduced-order template with identified physical parameters. A generalist parameter-conditioned policy is learned once at this junction; each imagined step rolls out the template then the world model, and training through each robot's template makes the policy body-aware.

Key Results

On seven simulated robots of five locomotion types, the generalist policy outperforms imitation-learning and diffusion-policy baselines by 3.8-10.6 percentage points in success rate and matches per-robot specialists without dedicated training, approaching its parameter-conditioned MPPI teacher within 1.4-2.8 points without an online map. After three minutes of teleoperated driving for identification, the synthetic-data-trained model transfers zero-shot to a real skid-steer robot unseen in training and navigates unseen indoor scenes.

Limitations

  • The approach assumes flat ground, where embodiment affects observations only through camera ego-motion.
  • Robot dynamics are represented by reduced-order templates with identified physical parameters, which may not capture full embodiment dynamics.
  • Real-robot deployment requires per-robot identification via teleoperated driving before zero-shot navigation.
