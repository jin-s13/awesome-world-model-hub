Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Identifies that introducing action learning can impair language-video alignment in world action models, and proposes studying data and supervision design to maintain cross-modal alignment.
  • Curates a large multi-source training pool of approximately 47,000 hours spanning general videos, egocentric videos, UMI demonstrations, and robot demonstrations.
  • Systematically compares language-video supervision, language-video-action supervision, and their joint use for world action model training, including analysis of language annotation quality and action supervision sources.
  • Provides a joint training recipe and architecture study showing that mixing egocentric videos and UMI demonstrations under a fixed training budget improves control generalization.
  • Develops the SWAM recipe that, after post-training only on RoboTwin Clean demonstrations, generalizes to harder RoboTwin settings and real-world tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline.

Methodology

The authors curate a dataset pool of about 47,000 hours from general videos, egocentric videos, UMI demonstrations, and robot demonstrations, then systematically study language-video supervision, language-video-action supervision, and their combination for world action models. They also analyze language annotation quality, action supervision sources, joint training recipes, and model architecture under a fixed training budget. The resulting SWAM recipe is post-trained on RoboTwin Clean demonstrations only and evaluated on RoboTwin Easy/Hard conditions and three real-world tasks.

Key Results

The final model post-trained only on RoboTwin Clean demonstrations achieves 88.10% success rate on Easy (Clean-to-Clean) and 61.56% on Hard (Clean-to-Random), and attains 40.00% average success rate across three real-world tasks compared with 23.33% for the strongest baseline. Mixing egocentric videos and UMI demonstrations improves control generalization, and scaling pretraining under the selected configuration brings further gains.
